Firm Foundation: MD to Healing

I study molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, lipid nanoparticle (LNP) drug delivery, and purinergic therapeutics targeting neuroinflammation. The goal of my research is to connect molecular-level understanding to real therapy.

The question I ask is simple: “how can a phenomenon at the molecular scale be carried forward into a therapy that actually helps a patient?”

I believe good nanomedicine is not found by chance but designed more rationally — once we understand how lipid composition, drug molecules, and the physical properties of the membrane interact.

My current work uses Martini coarse-grained MD simulation to study composition-dependent structural changes in LNPs, small-molecule loading and distribution, membrane organization, and drug–lipid interactions. The aim is to reduce trial and error at the bench and design more efficient drug delivery systems.

Three principles guide my research:

  • Connect molecular-level insight to therapy
  • Do research grounded in data and evidence
  • Keep people as the ultimate purpose of the work

Going forward, I want to advance simulation-guided nanomedicine design that bridges molecular modeling and clinical insight.

From molecular dynamics to healing — from molecules to medicine.
